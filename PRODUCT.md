# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Japanese-speaking programmers, students, and working developers who use ufcpp.net as a
reference while learning C#, .NET, algorithms, mathematics, and related computer-science
topics. They need dense technical material to remain readable, searchable, and familiar
across desktop and mobile devices.

## Product Purpose

Preserve and publish the ufcpp.net content archive as a deterministic static site without
losing the identity or navigational familiarity of the current site. Success means readers
can recognize the site immediately, move through long-form technical content efficiently,
and trust that code, formulas, tables, and historical pages remain usable.

## Brand Personality

Practical, approachable, and technical. The site should feel like a well-used technical
reference room: information-rich, direct, and recognizable rather than polished into a
generic product-marketing surface.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS landing pages with oversized hero copy, card grids, gradients, or glass effects.
- A dark-mode-first developer-tool redesign that replaces the established ufcpp.net palette.
- A minimal editorial redesign that sacrifices navigation density or the site's familiar logo.
- Decorative modernization that makes historical technical content feel detached from ufcpp.net.

## Design Principles

- Preserve recognition: the live ufcpp.net logo and canonical lavender-and-navy palette lead.
- Put reference work first: navigation, headings, code, tables, and related links stay compact.
- Modernize behavior, not identity: improve semantics, responsive layout, and accessibility
  without inventing a new visual brand.
- Keep technical content honest: decoration never competes with examples, formulas, or prose.
- Maintain deterministic output: visual assets and styles are generated consistently offline.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Meet WCAG 2.1 AA contrast and keyboard-focus expectations for core text and navigation.
Preserve semantic main, navigation, aside, and footer landmarks. Keep content usable at narrow
widths, allow wide code and tables to scroll, and avoid motion that requires a reduced-motion
alternative.
